# Nonlinear reference motion and bounded navigation

The nonlinear services define a finite synthetic plant and conditional continuous enclosures. They do not establish flight qualification, atmosphere/weather coverage, sensor calibration, or a completed closed-loop mission. GEKKO/IPOPT and Carter-Humi dynamics remain the nominal planning path. The nonlinear plant, sensing and admission services must be explicitly selected by mission orchestration.

## Coordinates, clock and forces

`orbexa.core.reference_frame.ReferenceClockFrame` is immutable. Its reference rotation obeys `C'=C Z`, with `Z p=(-p_y,p_x,0)`. Its positive rate `nu_ref(q)` defines physical seconds through `dt/dq=1/nu_ref`. The reference ellipse parameters are fixed scheduling/coordinate declarations, independent of later adaptive coefficient estimates. These axes are called `reference-orbit-frame-v1`, not the perturbed target LVLH.

Let `p=C^T(r_c-r_t)` and `w=dp/dq`. Then

    r_c = r_t + C p
    v_c = v_t + nu_ref C(w+Zp)
    r_t' = v_t/nu_ref
    v_t' = a_t/nu_ref
    p' = w
    w' = C^T(a_c-a_t)/nu_ref^2 - 2Zw - Z^2p
         - (nu_ref'/nu_ref)(w+Zp) + u.

Here the force difference excludes the applied command; physical command acceleration is `nu_ref^2 C u`. The six-state `HeldInputRequest` therefore holds the coordinate command, while physical direction/magnitude follow the declared reference schedule. This is distinct from inertial force/acceleration ZOH. Additional actuator error is a declared anomaly-acceleration inclusion in `request.acceleration_disturbance`.

`NonlinearModelBounds` supports central gravity, fixed-axis J2, and full vector isotropic quadratic drag. `ExponentialAtmosphere` declares positive scale height, nonnegative reference density, rigid atmospheric co-rotation and a constant bounded wind. Density, J2, mu, drag area/mass and atmosphere parameters may be finite intervals. Arbitrary numerical callbacks have no proof interpretation here. `radius_domain_m` applies to the entire target and chaser radius enclosures. Relative position/velocity limits are componentwise absolute bounds in m and m/rad.

Central and J2 differences use canceled inverse-power identities with `S-R=(2r.d+d.d)/(S+R)`. Differential drag similarly uses speed and density differences. This avoids subtracting wide absolute gravity fields. The same algebra functions are used with binary64 and Arb arithmetic. The numerical realization must be point-valued: choosing `model.midpoint_model()` is an explicit synthetic realization, not an implicit use of parameter truth.

Public core operations are:

- `clock.point(q)`: rotation, positive rate in 1/s, and logarithmic q derivative of rate.
- `clock.interval_bounds(q_interval)`: exact outward boxes for nine rotation entries, rate and rate derivative.
- `clock.elapsed_bounds(q_start,q_end, max_step=..., max_segments=...)`: finite-budget interval quadrature of physical duration.
- `clock.to_reference(cartesian_states,q,target_state)` and `to_inertial(relative_states,q,target_state)`: numerical frame transforms.
- `NonlinearRelativeDynamics(model,clock).target_rhs(q,target)`, `.relative_rhs(q,relative,target,u)`, and `.fleet_rhs(q,target,relative_states,commands)`: numerical q derivatives; the last returns target derivatives, relative derivatives and `dt/dq`.

## Sensor products and state estimation

`orbexa.estimation.navigation.SensorSuite` is the truth-side generator. Its `sample(q=...,physical_time_s=...,relative_states=...,target_rotation=...,target_angular_velocity=...,chaser_inertial_states=...)` produces immutable `SensorPacket`s. A controller receives packets, not this sampler or its truth arguments.

The optional last argument produces own-chaser GNSS packets; the target has no assumed cooperative GNSS. `infer_target_ephemeris(gnss_packet,relative_box,clock)` uses Arb to infer target position/velocity from chaser absolute measurements and relative geometry. Callers must wait until the packet's receipt event before relying on this inferred prior. Initial velocity uncertainty comes from a declared release/navigation prior and observations, not truth velocity.

Range/bearing products are processed target-COM geometry in the reference frame. Bearing components are perturbed independently and deliberately not renormalized, preserving their deterministic component support. The range support is derived from reported range and the calibration inequality, rather than leaking hidden true range through its metadata. A target-pose packet supplies a processed matrix/rate support; no raw image, point-cloud, natural-feature association or occlusion reconstruction is claimed. Range gates are implemented. An application needing visibility/FOV/occlusion must supply those observation guarantees before treating these products as a physical sensing qualification.

`StudySensorProfile` defaults are explicit illustrative values: range noise support `0.5 mm + 20 ppm * range`, an additional bounded persistent range bias of `0.5 mm`, bearing component support `30 microrad`, target rotation operator error `1e-3`, target rate component support `1e-4 rad/s`, chaser GNSS supports `1 m` and `0.005 m/s`, latency up to `0.05 s`, and no stochastic dropout by default. Finite dropout bursts and stochastic dropout are configurable. These numbers are study assumptions, not device specifications. Sensor packet support includes declared errors; numerical synthetic truth and sensor evaluation still need their own verified numerical-error allowance for a fully rigorous synthetic-sensing claim.

GNSS and target-rate packet supports additionally retain two ULPs of each reported binary64 value, added by exact rational arithmetic to the unchanged hardware error. These allowances use the reported measurement rather than hidden truth. They cover endpoint addition rounding without changing the noise draw, bias, seed, or hardware magnitude. Range/bearing and pose-matrix products retain their separate explicit numerical evaluation reserves. The small component allowances do not qualify arbitrary perception or sensing algorithms.

`BoundedNavigationFilter(initial_relative_boxes,target_ephemeris_box,clock,model,...)` is separate from the sampler. Its inputs are authoritative initial priors, `.predict(flows_by_agent,target_flow)` and `.consume(packets)`. A point estimate is the midpoint of the retained box; it is never a truth-centered measurement. The filter verifies flow/prior/model/clock identities, narrows position with packet support, handles delayed observations through finite certified history replay, and infers/constrains target ephemeris from GNSS/relative geometry. Out-of-history or unresolved inversion/replay retains the prior. Empty intersections mark `INCONSISTENT`. A later sensor-model change requires an explicit declared transition; optional `sensor_fingerprints` can bind the allowed set from construction.

Velocity contraction is obtained from two acquired position boxes and the certified acceleration history:

    w(q1) = (p(q1)-p(q0))/(q1-q0)
            + integral((s-q0)*w'(s),s=q0..q1)/(q1-q0).

All segment acceleration boxes and nonnegative integral weights are exact. Delayed constraints are replayed to the current event before intersection. This reduces velocity uncertainty without a truth velocity packet. Dropouts lengthen prediction; they never synthesize perfect observations. Receipt timestamps are checked against the retained physical-time enclosure, and observations are never relabeled as acquired now.

The filter's target pose/rate propagation requires explicit whole-window rate and angular-acceleration bounds. Its initial defaults are study priors, not an independently proven rigid-body attitude model. A dedicated attitude inclusion or a separately justified physical bound must supply those hypotheses for occupied-geometry admission.

The pose point now advances with midpoint observed body rate and the prescribed reference-frame motion. Its support adds rate uncertainty, bounded angular acceleration, clock uncertainty, and an Arb enclosure of the floating rotation evaluation error. A delayed packet therefore does not leave its point estimate frozen at acquisition. Body-rate packet components are in target-body axes; its rotation maps that body into the reference axes.

A successful short numerical attitude replay is not that gate.

`simulation.nonlinear_target_bounds.preview_error_bounds(..., target_flow=full_target_certificate)` bounds moving docking goals at the declared planning nodes. The full target certificate must match the attitude forecast's target/model/clock/start/width identities, including physical-time witnesses. Direct quaternion point images remain an independent conservative calculation. With directly supplied initial pose support, a second comparison preserves the physical rotation constraint instead of treating every quaternion-box corner as a possible proper rotation.

Let `R(t)` be the physical body-to-inertial rotation, `G0` an exactly unit-quaternion normalized declared reference, and `omega0` any fixed observed numerical body-rate center. Define `G(t)=G0*exp([omega0]x*(t-t0))`. Since both `R` and the exponential are proper,

    ||R(t)-G(t)||op <= Gamma0 + integral(||omega(s)-omega0|| ds).

This follows by differentiating `R(t)*exp(-[omega0]x*(t-t0))`; the two exterior rotation factors have operator norm one. `Gamma0` adds the directly declared initial observation operator error and an Arb Frobenius bound for its projection onto `G0`. Every integral piece uses the physical body-rate support of the unchanged nonlinear Euler/Picard certificate and a positive-clock duration enclosure on an exact common cut. Arb Rodrigues evaluation, including a separate zero-spin branch, then encloses `C(q)^T*G(t(q))-Rpreview_float(q)`. Adding that explicit discrepancy gives `Gamma(q)` about the defined numerical preview. No numerical preview integration accuracy, hidden attitude sample, future observation, or nominal attitude ODE is assumed.

For a body-fixed docking point `b`, a numerical preview matrix/rate `Rbar,omegabar`, positive exact clock lower bound `nu_min`, and point rate-error norm `delta_omega`, the resulting goal bounds are

    E_position <= Gamma*||b|| + epsilon_position_eval
    E_velocity <= [Gamma+(Gamma*||omegabar||+delta_omega)/nu_min]*||b||
                  + epsilon_velocity_eval.

The evaluation defects enclose the actual numerical `docking_state` output against exact declared matrix/rate/clock arithmetic. In the rate term, `(Ractual-Rbar)*omegabar + Ractual*(omegaactual-omegabar)` uses the physical `||Ractual||op=1`. The minimum of this bound and each independent quaternion-point bound is valid for the same source-bound physical support. `proper_rotation_comparison=False` retains the quaternion-based alternative. None of these pointwise bounds narrows whole-window geometry sweeps.

Before a planning context is returned, a supplied certificate sink acknowledges one `kind='moving_goal_forecast'` event containing the complete target and attitude certificates, exact rate/clock comparison trace, node supports, numerical preview values and source identities. The retained streamed context contains the charged scalar bounds and forecast/comparison IDs; it drops full trace/point/segment payloads. Without a sink, the context explicitly retains that full witness in memory. A failed archive acknowledgment prevents planning-context return. This is prospective bounded attitude/goal prediction under the declared model, not a docking or full-mission completion claim.

## Declared command-dependent actuation support

`CommandDependentActuationPrior(gain_error,tilt_rad,bias_abs,round_relative,round_absolute,command_abs_limit)` is a typed prospective study declaration supplied through the executor's `actuation_prior` argument. For a known command `u`, it produces

    eta_i(u) = theta*(1+g)*sqrt_upper(sum(u_j^2)) + g*abs(u_i) + b
               + round_relative*sqrt_upper(sum(u_j^2)) + round_absolute.

For a proper tilt `R` and bounded diagonal gain `D`, `R*D-I=(R-I)*D+(D-I)`, `||R-I||2<=theta`, and `||D||2<=1+g` give this component bound. Its magnitude is determined by the declared prior and commanded input, independently of the actual realized matrix. Default study declarations are `g=.001`, `theta=.001 rad`, `b=.2 m/rad²`, numerical reserves `1e-10*||u||+1e-9`, and component command cap `100000 m/rad²`. The maximum component allowance at that cap is about `273.5784 m/rad²`, within the existing global `300` support. These remain synthetic study assumptions.

The constructor independently validates the supported `AffineActuatorRealization`. It clips the actual matrix diagonal into the declared `[1-g,1+g]` range to obtain a proof-only `Dproxy`, and checks exact outward row norms of `M-Dproxy` plus conservative binary64 evaluation error against the fixed declared slope. A gamma16/underflow allowance covers the supported affine evaluation. This establishes the needed component envelope and can cover a superset of exact proper `R*D` matrices; it does not identify an actual tilt/gain decomposition. The record retains the exact matrix/bias, source-bound prior and validation identities. Changing either realization or prior after validation refuses before motion. Neither the validation row norms nor physical truth set the controller's error allowance.

The NLP forcing, performance admission input boxes, fallback invariance/geometry and resource charges retain the global `300` support. Navigation additionally propagates a separate validated flow using `command +/- eta(command)` after checking it is a subset of the admitted box. Stage archives retain both `flows` and `navigation_flows`; records retain `admission_input_supports`, typed `navigation_input_supports`, both flow ID maps and the prior/validation identities. `navigation_prediction_input_supports` names the boxes actually used by prediction. If a subset proof cannot be established while a complete admitted flow remains available, prediction conservatively uses that full flow and records the wider request explicitly.
